FDA Recall Risk & Severity Analysis (2024-2025)

SQL • Tableau • Excel • Risk Analytics

Executive Summary

  • This project analyzes U.S. FDA recall data from 2024–2025 to identify trends in recall frequency, classification severity, and affected product categories.

  • The analysis reveals that 2025 did not experience more recalls — it experienced more severe recalls, with food products accounting for the highest proportion of high-risk recalls.

  • These findings help manufacturers and regulators identify high-risk segments and improve preventive monitoring strategies.

Business Problem

How did recall risk and severity change from 2024 to 2025, and where should regulators focus preventive efforts?

Key Findings

  • Total recalls increased slightly (2.4%).

  • Class I recalls increased 15.4% year-over-year.

  • Food contamination was the dominant driver.

  • California showed the highest year-over-year increase.

Business Impact

2025 saw higher public health risk despite stable overall recall volume.

Business Context & Objectives

Why It Matters


  • Product recalls can result in financial loss, brand damage, and public health risk.

  • This analysis aims to identify patterns in recall severity and frequency to support proactive risk management and regulatory monitoring.

Target Stakeholders


  • Primary: FDA Regulatory & Compliance Officers

  • Secondary: Food Manufacturers & Quality Assurance Teams

Project Objectives

  • Understand recall frequency trends between 2024 and 2025.

  • Determine whether the most common recall classification changed year-over-year.

  • Evaluate differences in monthly recall trends between 2024 and 2025.

  • Identify which states experienced the largest increases or decreases in recalls.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Total Recall Volume

Definition: Total number of FDA recall events per year.


  • 2024: 1,539 recalls

  • 2025: 1,576 recalls

  • Year-over-Year Change: +37 recalls (+2.4%)

Why it matters:

Measures overall recall activity and regulatory workload. While volume remained similar, small increases may signal early shifts in compliance trends.



High-Risk Recall Rate (Class I %)

Definition: Percentage of total recalls classified as Class I (highest severity).


  • 2024: 43% (667 of 1,539 recalls)

  • 2025: 49% (770 of 1,576 recalls)

  • Change: +6 percentage points

  • Class I volume increase: +103 recalls (+15.4%)

Why it matters:

Indicates whether recalls are becoming more serious. Although total recalls only rose 2.4%, high-risk recalls increased significantly, showing a shift toward greater public health exposure.



Recall Closure Rate

Definition: Percentage of recalls that were terminated (officially resolved).


  • 2024: 88% terminated (1,358 of 1,539)

  • 2025: 45% terminated (703 of 1,576)

  • Ongoing in 2025: 49% (778 recalls)

  • Change: −43 percentage points in termination rate

Why it matters:

Measures operational efficiency and resolution speed. The sharp drop in closure rate suggests a growing backlog or slower recall resolution in 2025.



Seasonal Spike Index

Definition: Measures how much the highest recall month exceeds the average monthly baseline.


  • 2025 Monthly Average: 131 recalls

  • Peak Month (June 2025): 303 recalls

  • Spike Index: 2.3× above average

Why it matters:

Identifies seasonal surges in recall activity. Significant spikes may require temporary increases in monitoring and inspection resources.

Data Preparation & Cleaning

  • Standardized inconsistent text fields (e.g., recall reasons and state names) to prevent fragmented groupings in aggregate queries.

  • Created derived fields (recall year, recall month) to support time-based aggregations.

  • Removed non-essential metadata columns to streamline query performance and focus analysis on severity, geography, and recall cause.

  • Reviewed null or missing state values to ensure accurate state-level comparisons.

FDA Recall Class Definitions

  • Class I – High risk; reasonable probability of serious health consequences or death.

  • Class II – Temporary or medically reversible health effects.

  • Class III – Unlikely to cause adverse health consequences.


Execute Query 
-- This query breaks down FDA recalls by recall class
-- for the years 2024 and 2025

SELECT

    YEAR(recall_date) AS recall_year,
    recall_class,
    COUNT(*) AS recall_count

FROM fda_recalls

WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024, 2025)

GROUP BY
    YEAR(recall_date),
    recall_class

ORDER BY
    recall_year,
    recall_count DESC;
  • Total recalls remained consistent year-over-year (+2.4%, from 1,539 to 1,576), but severity increased in 2025.

  • Class I recalls rose significantly from 667 to 770, increasing their share from 43% to 49%. Meanwhile, Class II recalls declined from 805 to 718.

Takeaway

2025 did not see more recalls overall — it saw more high-risk recalls.



Execute Query 
--This query find the total recalls per month in 2024 and in 2025
 
 SELECT
		YEAR(recall_date) AS recall_year,
		DATENAME(MONTH, recall_date) AS recall_month,
		MONTH(recall_date) AS month_number,
		COUNT(*) AS recall_count
FROM fda_recalls
WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024, 2025)
GROUP BY
		YEAR(recall_date),
		DATENAME(MONTH, recall_date),
		MONTH(recall_date)
ORDER BY recall_year, month_number;
  • Recalls showed clear seasonality across 2024–2025, averaging approximately 130 recalls per month (3,115 total).

  • Activity peaked in June 2025 (303 recalls) and reached a low in November 2025 (63 recalls).

  • While 2024 remained relatively flat (147 to 145 recalls), 2025 trended upward from January to December (99 to 137, +38), indicating stronger momentum during the second half of the year.

Takeaway

Recalls followed a seasonal cycle, with 2025 showing a more pronounced late-year acceleration.



Execute Query 
--This query gives the top ten recall reasons using windows functions and CTE's

WITH recall_reasons as (
SELECT
		YEAR(recall_date) as recall_year,
		recall_reason,
		COUNT(*) AS total_recalls,
		RANK() OVER(PARTITION BY YEAR(recall_date) ORDER BY Count(*) DESC ) AS reason_rank
FROM fda_recalls
WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024,2025)
GROUP BY YEAR(recall_date), recall_reason
)
SELECT
		recall_year,
		recall_reason,
		total_recalls
FROM recall_reasons
WHERE reason_rank <=5
ORDER BY recall_year, recall_reason DESC;
  • Recall volume remained nearly identical year-over-year (+3%), but contamination risks drove activity.

  • The primary drivers were pathogen-related recalls, led by Listeria contamination on food contact surfaces (86 recalls in 2025).

  • Salmonella-related incidents were also significant, including ingredient-level contamination (e.g., cucumbers linked to sushi recalls).

Takeaway

While overall recall volume changed minimally, food safety risks—particularly environmental and ingredient contamination—remained the dominant trigger.



Execute Query 
--This query shows total recalls by state 
in 2024 and 2025 with CTE and case statements

WITH recall_state_year_counts AS (
select 
		recall_state,
		YEAR(recall_date) AS recall_year,
		COUNT(*) AS recall_count
FROM fda_recalls
WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024,2025)
GROUP BY 
		recall_state, YEAR(recall_date)
	)
SELECT
		recall_state,
		SUM(CASE WHEN recall_year = 2024 THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END) AS recalls_2024,
		SUM(CASE WHEN recall_year = 2025 THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END) AS recalls_2025,
		SUM(CASE WHEN recall_year = 2025 THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END) -
		SUM(CASE WHEN recall_year = 2024 THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END) AS yoy_change
FROM recall_state_year_counts
GROUP BY recall_state
ORDER BY yoy_change DESC;
  • Recall activity in 2025 was driven by state-level outliers rather than nationwide shifts.

  • California emerged as the #1 recall state in 2025.

  • The top five states with increases each added roughly 50–120 additional recalls, accounting for most of the national growth.

  • Conversely, the top five states with declines reduced recalls by 60–140 cases each, reflecting substantial year-over-year drops rather than minor improvements.

Takeaway

National recall trends were shaped by a small group of high-impact states, not broad changes across the country.

Business Interpretation

2025 saw increased public health risk despite stable total recall volume.

Limitations

  • The analysis includes only recalls that were classified in the source dataset.

  • The FDA dashboard is updated on a rolling basis, so counts may change as new records are published or existing records are revised.

  • The time window is limited to two years (2024–2025), which may not capture longer-term patterns or rare events.

Recommendations / Next Steps

  • Implement seasonal risk modeling to anticipate recall surges in Q2 (June 2025 recorded 303 recalls, the highest monthly spike).

  • Prioritize inspections for contamination-related issues (8 of the top 10 recall reasons were potential contamination cases).

  • Allocate more resources to Class I recall prevention (Class I recalls increased from 667 to 770 year-over-year).

Next Analytical Steps

  • Build a classification model to predict the likelihood a recall will be Class I based on product type, reason, and geography.

  • Develop a seasonal forecast of monthly recall volume to better staff inspections ahead of Q2 spikes.

  • Cluster states by recall profile (volume, severity, drivers) to prioritize regulatory outreach.

Conclusion

  • The 2025 FDA recall landscape reveals a critical shift: recalls didn't increase in volume, but they became significantly more dangerous.

  • Contamination—particularly from Listeria and Salmonella—emerged as the dominant systemic threat, while high-risk Class I recalls rose from 43% to 49% of all cases.

  • Risk concentration in a handful of states suggests that targeted, severity-based monitoring would be more effective than broad nationwide surveillance.
