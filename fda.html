FDA Recall Class Definitions

  • Class I – High risk; reasonable probability of serious health consequences or death.
  • Class II – Temporary or medically reversible health effects.
  • Class III – Unlikely to cause adverse health consequences.

Recall Classification Analysis

Execute Query 
-- Break down FDA recalls by class for 2024 and 2025

SELECT
    YEAR(recall_date) AS recall_year,
    recall_class,
    COUNT(*) AS recall_count
FROM fda_recalls
WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024, 2025)
GROUP BY
    YEAR(recall_date),
    recall_class
ORDER BY
    recall_year,
    recall_count DESC;
  • Total recalls remained relatively consistent year over year, increasing 2.4% from 1,539 to 1,576.
  • Class I recalls increased from 667 to 770, raising their share from approximately 43% to 49%.
  • Class II recalls declined from 805 to 718.

Takeaway

Overall recall volume changed only slightly, while the number of high-severity Class I recalls increased substantially.

Monthly Recall Trends

Execute Query 
-- Calculate monthly recall counts for 2024 and 2025

SELECT
    YEAR(recall_date) AS recall_year,
    DATENAME(MONTH, recall_date) AS recall_month,
    MONTH(recall_date) AS month_number,
    COUNT(*) AS recall_count
FROM fda_recalls
WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024, 2025)
GROUP BY
    YEAR(recall_date),
    DATENAME(MONTH, recall_date),
    MONTH(recall_date)
ORDER BY
    recall_year,
    month_number;
  • Recall activity varied throughout the year, with approximately 130 recalls per month across the two-year period.
  • Activity peaked in June 2025 at 303 recalls and reached a low of 63 recalls in November 2025.
  • Monthly patterns differed between years, highlighting the importance of examining trends beyond annual totals.

Takeaway

Monthly analysis reveals spikes and fluctuations that annual totals alone can conceal.

Recall Reasons Analysis

Execute Query 
-- Rank the most common recall reasons by year

WITH recall_reasons AS (
    SELECT
        YEAR(recall_date) AS recall_year,
        recall_reason,
        COUNT(*) AS total_recalls,
        RANK() OVER (
            PARTITION BY YEAR(recall_date)
            ORDER BY COUNT(*) DESC
        ) AS reason_rank
    FROM fda_recalls
    WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024, 2025)
    GROUP BY
        YEAR(recall_date),
        recall_reason
)
SELECT
    recall_year,
    recall_reason,
    total_recalls
FROM recall_reasons
WHERE reason_rank <= 5
ORDER BY
    recall_year,
    total_recalls DESC;
  • Recall reasons help identify recurring product safety issues that may warrant additional attention.
  • Pathogen-related concerns, including Listeria and Salmonella, were important drivers of food recall activity.
  • Reviewing the most frequent reasons by year helps reveal which risks remain persistent and which change over time.

Takeaway

Identifying recurring contamination-related risks can help inform quality assurance, preventive controls, and regulatory monitoring.

State-Level Recall Analysis

Execute Query 
-- Compare recall counts by state across both years

WITH recall_state_year_counts AS (
    SELECT
        recall_state,
        YEAR(recall_date) AS recall_year,
        COUNT(*) AS recall_count
    FROM fda_recalls
    WHERE YEAR(recall_date) IN (2024, 2025)
    GROUP BY
        recall_state,
        YEAR(recall_date)
)
SELECT
    recall_state,
    SUM(
        CASE WHEN recall_year = 2024
        THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END
    ) AS recalls_2024,
    SUM(
        CASE WHEN recall_year = 2025
        THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END
    ) AS recalls_2025,
    SUM(
        CASE WHEN recall_year = 2025
        THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END
    ) -
    SUM(
        CASE WHEN recall_year = 2024
        THEN recall_count ELSE 0 END
    ) AS yoy_change
FROM recall_state_year_counts
GROUP BY recall_state
ORDER BY yoy_change DESC;
  • State-level comparisons highlight geographic differences in reported recall activity.
  • California emerged as a significant area of interest in the year-over-year analysis.
  • Comparing both increases and decreases helps distinguish states driving overall growth from those experiencing declines.

Takeaway

Geographic analysis helps identify where reported recall activity changed most and where further investigation may be warranted.

Business Interpretation

The analysis demonstrates why recall volume should not be evaluated in isolation. Classification severity, recall causes, monthly patterns, and geographic differences provide additional context for understanding food safety risks.

For regulators and quality assurance teams, these findings can support more targeted investigation, preventive controls, and resource planning.

Limitations

  • The analysis includes only recalls represented in the source dataset.
  • The FDA dashboard is updated on a rolling basis, so counts may change as records are published or revised.
  • The two-year period (2024–2025) may not capture longer-term patterns or less frequent events.
  • Observed associations do not establish the underlying cause of differences between states, months, or recall classifications.

Recommendations / Next Steps

  • Investigate recurring seasonal spikes to determine whether additional monitoring resources may be needed during peak periods.
  • Prioritize review of recurring contamination-related issues using recall frequency and classification severity.
  • Examine the drivers behind changes in Class I recalls to identify opportunities for stronger preventive controls.
  • Validate state-level differences against reporting coverage and product-category mix before making resource allocation decisions.

Next Analytical Steps

Conclusion
