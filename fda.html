Executive Summary

This project analyzes U.S. FDA recall data from 2024–2025 to identify trends in recall frequency, classification severity, and affected product categories.


The analysis reveals that 2025 did not experience more recalls—it experienced more severe recalls, with food products accounting for the highest proportion of high-risk recalls.


These findings help manufacturers and regulators identify high-risk segments and improve preventive monitoring strategies.

Business Problem

How did recall risk and severity change between 2024 and 2025, and where should regulators focus preventive efforts?

Key Findings

Total Recall Volume Increased

Total recalls increased by 2.4% year-over-year.


High-Risk Recalls Increased

Class I recalls increased 15.4% year-over-year.


Food Contamination Dominated Recall Activity

Food contamination remained the dominant recall driver.


California Saw the Largest Increase

California recorded the largest year-over-year increase in recalls.

Recommendations

  • Implement seasonal risk modeling for Q2 recall spikes.

  • Prioritize contamination-related inspection programs.

  • Allocate more resources toward Class I recall prevention.

Conclusion

The 2025 FDA recall landscape reveals a critical shift: recalls did not increase substantially in volume, but they became significantly more severe.


Contamination emerged as the dominant risk driver while high-risk Class I recalls increased from 43% to 49% of all cases.
