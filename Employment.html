Executive Summary

Understanding labor market trends is essential for workforce planning, economic forecasting, and strategic decision-making. This project analyzed benchmark-revised employment data from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) to uncover employment concentration, industry growth patterns, workforce stability, and seasonal hiring trends across major sectors of the U.S. economy. The final deliverable was an interactive Tableau dashboard designed to transform labor market data into actionable business insights.

Business Problem

Organizations, economists, and workforce planners rely on labor market data to understand economic conditions, workforce demand, and industry performance. The objective was to identify which industries employ the most workers, which sectors are growing or declining, how concentrated employment is across industries, and which industries demonstrate strong seasonal hiring behavior.

ETL Process

Extract

Retrieved benchmark-revised employment data and imported raw records into a SQL staging environment.

Transform

Removed duplicates, standardized industry names, handled missing values, created date dimensions, calculated growth metrics, generated rankings, and performed volatility analysis.

Load

Loaded transformed data into SQL Server and connected Tableau to create an interactive reporting layer.

Data Model

assets/employment-data-model.png

Implemented a dimensional star schema consisting of a fact_employment table and supporting industry and date dimensions to support reporting and trend analysis.

Key Findings

assets/employment-findings.png
  • Healthcare represented approximately 21% of employment.
  • Healthcare recorded the strongest growth rate at 3.5%.
  • More than 41% of employment was concentrated within two industries.
  • Information experienced the largest decline at 2.1%.
  • Construction, Retail Trade, and Leisure & Hospitality displayed the strongest seasonality.

Recommendations

  • Monitor Healthcare as a leading indicator of labor market expansion.
  • Account for seasonality when evaluating workforce trends.
  • Investigate declining sectors for structural shifts in workforce demand.
  • Expand analysis with unemployment, inflation, and GDP indicators.
